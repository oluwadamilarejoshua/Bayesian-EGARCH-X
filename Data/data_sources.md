## Source 1: World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI)

**Provider:** The World Bank Group  
**Database:** World Development Indicators  
**Access URL:** https://databank.worldbank.org/source/world-development-indicators  
**API base URL:** https://api.worldbank.org/v2/  
**Citation format:**  
> World Bank (2024). *World Development Indicators*. The World Bank Group. Retrieved from https://databank.worldbank.org/source/world-development-indicators

### Indicators Used

#### 1. Official Exchange Rate — `PA.NUS.FCRF`
- **Full name:** Official exchange rate (LCU per US$, period average)
- **Definition:** The official exchange rate refers to the exchange rate determined by national authorities or the rate determined in the legally sanctioned exchange market. It is calculated as an annual average based on monthly averages.
- **Units:** Nigerian Naira (NGN) per 1 U.S. Dollar (USD)
- **Frequency:** Annual
- **Coverage in dataset:** 1980–2020 (41 observations, no missing values)
- **File column:** `exchange_rate_NGN_per_USD`

#### 2. GDP Growth — `NY.GDP.MKTP.KD.ZG`
- **Full name:** GDP growth (annual %)
- **Definition:** Annual percentage growth rate of GDP at market prices based on constant local currency. Aggregates are based on constant 2015 U.S. dollars.
- **Units:** Percent (%)
- **Frequency:** Annual
- **Coverage in dataset:** 1980–2020 (41 observations, no missing values)
- **File column:** `gdp_growth_pct`

#### 3. Total Debt Service as % of Exports — `DT.TDS.DECT.EX.ZS`
- **Full name:** Total debt service (% of exports of goods, services and primary income)
- **Definition:** Total debt service is the sum of principal repayments and interest actually paid in currency, goods, or services on long-term debt, interest paid on short-term debt, and repayments to the IMF.
- **Units:** Percent of exports of goods, services, and primary income (%)
- **Frequency:** Annual
- **Coverage in dataset:** 1980–2020 (41 observations, no missing values)
- **File column:** `debt_service_pct_exports`
- **Note:** Preferred over the raw USD figure (`DT.TDS.DECT.CD`) because it is a relative measure that adjusts for country size and external exposure, making it more appropriate for cross-period comparisons and regression analysis.

#### 4. Total Debt Service in USD — `DT.TDS.DECT.CD`
- **Full name:** Total debt service (current US$)
- **Definition:** As above; reported in current U.S. dollars.
- **Units:** Current USD
- **Frequency:** Annual
- **Coverage in dataset:** 1980–2020 (41 observations, no missing values)
- **File column:** `debt_service_USD`
- **Note:** Retained for reference. Requires log-transformation before use in regression. The % of exports column is the recommended primary variable.

## Source 2: IMF International Financial Statistics via DBnomics (RECOMMENDED)

**Provider:** International Monetary Fund  
**Database:** International Financial Statistics (IFS)  
**Series:** `M.NG.ENDA_XDC_USD_RATE` — Monthly, Nigeria, Exchange Rate Domestic Currency per USD, Period Average  
**Access via DBnomics:** https://db.nomics.world/IMF/IFS/M.NG.ENDA_XDC_USD_RATE  
**R access:** `rdbnomics::rdb("IMF", "IFS", mask = "M.NG.ENDA_XDC_USD_RATE")`  
**Citation format:**  
> International Monetary Fund (2024). *International Financial Statistics [IFS]*. IMF Data. Washington D.C. Accessed via DBnomics. https://db.nomics.world/IMF/IFS/M.NG.ENDA_XDC_USD_RATE

### Why this is the recommended source

This series is the authoritative official exchange rate used by the IMF, sourced directly from the Central Bank of Nigeria. It is monthly, covers January 1957 to present, has no missing values in the 1980–2020 window, and is the same underlying source that the World Bank's annual WDI indicator PA.NUS.FCRF aggregates for its annual figures. The `rdbnomics` R package provides free, unauthenticated access.

## Source 3: Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis — FRED (Fallback)

**Provider:** Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis  
**Database:** Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED)  
**Citation format:**  
> Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (2024). *FRED Economic Data*. https://fred.stlouisfed.org

### IMPORTANT — Series ID Correction

**`EXNAUS` does not exist on FRED.** This was the incorrect series ID used in the original script, which caused the 404 error. The correct FRED series IDs for Nigeria are:

| Series ID | Full Name | Coverage | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FXRATENGA618NUPN` | Exchange Rate to U.S. Dollar for Nigeria | 1950–2010 | IMF IFS source, official rate |
| `XRNCUSNGA618NRUG` | Exchange Rate (market+estimated) for Nigeria | 1950–2023 | Blended market/estimated rate |

**R access:**
```r
quantmod::getSymbols("FXRATENGA618NUPN", src = "FRED")
# or
quantmod::getSymbols("XRNCUSNGA618NRUG", src = "FRED")
```

**Caveat:** Confirm the frequency of these series before using. If the number of observations divided by the number of years is less than 6, the series is annual, not monthly. The script `data_fetch_monthly.R` includes an automatic frequency check and will warn you if this occurs. If either FRED series turns out to be annual, use the DBnomics/IMF IFS approach (Source 2 above).

## Source 3: Yahoo Finance (Reference Only — DO NOT USE as Primary)

**Provider:** Yahoo Finance  
**Ticker:** NGNUSD=X  
**File:** `nigeria_monthly_exr_yahoo_partial.csv`

### IMPORTANT CAVEATS

This file is retained for reference only and **must not be used** as the primary exchange rate source. Reasons:

1. **Parallel market rate:** The Yahoo Finance NGNUSD=X ticker reflects rates from the parallel/interbank foreign exchange market, not the Central Bank of Nigeria official rate. During periods of capital controls (notably 2015–2017, when Nigeria maintained an official peg while the parallel market traded at a significant premium), Yahoo values diverge substantially from WDI official rates. For example, in 2016, Yahoo-derived rates imply approximately 150 NGN/USD while the WDI official rate was 253 NGN/USD.

2. **Coverage gap:** Yahoo data only reaches back to approximately December 2003. It does not cover the pre-liberalisation era of 1980–1999, which is critical for this study.

3. **Bimonthly sampling:** The Yahoo data is sampled every two months, not monthly, resulting in only ~102 observations over 17 years instead of the expected 204.

4. **Academic citation:** Yahoo Finance is not an academically citable source for official exchange rate data in a peer-reviewed economics paper.

**Use IMF IFS via rdbnomics (Source 2 above) or FRED series `FXRATENGA618NUPN` / `XRNCUSNGA618NRUG` instead. `EXNAUS` does not exist.**

## Interpolation of Annual Macro Data to Monthly

GDP growth and debt service are only available at annual frequency from the World Bank. To combine them with monthly exchange rate data, we apply the **Denton-Cholette temporal disaggregation method** via the R package `tempdisagg`.

The Denton-Cholette method distributes annual aggregates to higher frequency while minimizing the sum of squared differences between consecutive monthly values (movement preservation criterion). This ensures the monthly interpolated series:
- Sums (or averages) back to the original annual figure
- Preserves the year-to-year movement pattern
- Does not introduce spurious volatility

**Reference:**  
> Sax, C., & Steiner, P. (2013). Temporal disaggregation of time series. *The R Journal*, 5(2), 80–87. https://doi.org/10.32614/RJ-2013-028

> Denton, F. T. (1971). Adjustment of monthly or quarterly series to annual totals: An approach based on quadratic minimization. *Journal of the American Statistical Association*, 66(333), 99–102.

## Notes for Paper Writing (Methods Section)

When describing the data in the revised paper, use language similar to the following:

**Annual data paragraph:**
> Annual data for the official Nigerian naira/U.S. dollar exchange rate (indicator PA.NUS.FCRF), GDP growth (NY.GDP.MKTP.KD.ZG), and total debt service as a percentage of exports of goods and services (DT.TDS.DECT.EX.ZS) were obtained from the World Bank World Development Indicators database for the period 1980 to 2020.

**Monthly data paragraph (if using monthly frequency):**
> Monthly official exchange rate data (Nigerian Naira per U.S. Dollar) were obtained from the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis FRED database (series EXNAUS), sourced from the IMF International Financial Statistics, covering January 1980 to December 2020 (492 observations). GDP growth and debt service, which are only available at annual frequency, were disaggregated to monthly frequency using the Denton-Cholette temporal disaggregation method (Denton 1971; Sax & Steiner 2013), implemented via the `tempdisagg` package in R (version X.X).
